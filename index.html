Manufacturing Agentic Transformation Accelerator

A plant runs on physics, takt time, and people who can hear a bearing failing before the sensor does. Agents earn their place on that floor only if they respect the boundary between information and action: they may watch, predict, draft, and recommend, but the physical world moves on human authority. This accelerator packages the patterns, the readiness assessment, and the governance pack a manufacturer needs to run agents from the edge to the enterprise on the Microsoft stack.

A working reference artifact by Greg Lucas, founder of AIccountable and creator of agentlint. Every pattern here is designed around one rule: agents advise and prepare, humans authorize anything that touches a machine, a line, or a shipment.

Independent portfolio project. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Microsoft.

Why manufacturing, why now

Manufacturing margin hides in unplanned downtime, scrap, schedule churn, and supplier surprises. The data to see all four earlier already exists, in historians, MES, maintenance logs, and supplier emails, but it lives in systems that were never designed to talk to each other, and the experts who read it are retiring faster than they can be replaced. Agents connect the signals and draft the response. The hard part is not the model. It is the boundary: operational technology and information technology have different failure costs, and an agent that confuses them is a safety incident waiting for a shift change.

This site runs that play in the open. Four patterns are mapped service by service to the Microsoft stack: Azure AI Foundry for agents, Azure IoT Operations and the industrial edge for plant data, Dynamics 365 for maintenance and production workflows, Microsoft Fabric for the data foundation, and Microsoft Defender for IoT guarding the OT side. An interactive assessment scores your operation across the Identify to Prove arc, and the governance section maps agentlint style checks to OT and IT safety controls and plant floor audit requirements.

Four transformation patterns

Each pattern names the operational outcome, the agent design, and the Microsoft services it runs on. Every pattern ships with the same governance spine: OT and IT zones segmented with agents reading across the boundary through governed brokers, never writing into control systems, human authorization on every physical world action, and plant floor audit trails that survive a customer or regulator review.

1. Quality Inspection Agent

Vision and data inspection at line speed: the agent reads camera and sensor feeds at the station, compares against the golden standard and process parameters, flags suspect parts with the evidence frame attached, and drafts the nonconformance record. Quality engineers disposition product. The agent never passes or scraps product on its own authority.

Outcome: defect escapes fall as issues are caught at the station instead of the dock, inspectors review evidence instead of watching every part, and every flag is auditable.

Stack: Azure AI Foundry with Azure OpenAI vision models for defect reasoning, Azure IoT Operations at the edge for camera and sensor ingestion, Azure Arc managed edge deployment, Microsoft Fabric for quality data products and SPC analytics, Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management quality orders for disposition workflow, Microsoft Defender for IoT for device and network protection.

2. Predictive Maintenance Copilot

A copilot for maintenance teams that earns the name: it watches vibration, temperature, and runtime patterns, correlates them with work order history and the failure library, and drafts prioritized work orders with the likely fault, the parts list, and the safe procedure attached. Planners approve, schedule, and assign. Wrenches stay human.

Outcome: unplanned downtime falls as failures get scheduled instead of suffered, planner time shifts from triage to planning, and tribal knowledge becomes a library instead of a retirement risk.

Stack: Azure IoT Operations and Azure Stream Analytics heritage edge processing for telemetry, Azure AI Foundry for the copilot and failure reasoning, Microsoft Fabric for the equipment data foundation, Dynamics 365 Field Service and Supply Chain Management asset management for work orders, Microsoft 365 Copilot surfaces for planner interaction, Defender for IoT across the sensor estate.

3. Production Scheduling Agent

The schedule is a negotiation the plant re-runs every disruption. The agent drafts it: it reads orders, capacity, material availability, and changeover constraints, proposes a schedule with the trade offs named, and re-drafts on disruption with the impact of each option quantified. The production manager owns the final schedule and every expedite decision.

Outcome: schedule drafting drops from hours to minutes, disruptions get options instead of chaos, and on time delivery improves without heroics.

Stack: Azure AI Foundry for the scheduling agent and constraint reasoning, Microsoft Fabric for orders, capacity, and inventory data products, Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management planning and production control as the system of record, Copilot Studio for the planner conversation, Microsoft 365 Copilot in Teams for disruption alerts, Entra ID for role based access.

4. Supplier Risk Monitor

An always on watch over the supply base: the monitor reads supplier financial signals, delivery performance, quality escapes, geopolitical and weather feeds, and the plant's own exposure, then briefs procurement with emerging risks ranked by production impact and draft mitigation options. Buyers decide. The monitor never contacts a supplier or moves an order.

Outcome: supplier surprises surface weeks earlier, single source exposure is quantified instead of rumored, and procurement walks into negotiations with evidence.

Stack: Azure AI Foundry for the monitoring agent, Azure OpenAI for document and news reasoning, Microsoft Fabric for supplier and spend data products, Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management vendor data, Azure AI Search over contracts and scorecards, Microsoft Purview for data governance, Defender for Cloud for platform protection.

          The first 90 days, the way a plant transformation team would run it

          • Days 1 to 30, Identify and charter: run the census across plants with operations, EHS, quality, and OT security in the room. Pick one pattern, quality inspection or predictive maintenance, draw its physical action boundary in writing, and sign its charter before any hardware is touched.
          • Days 31 to 60, Design and prove the data: stand up the edge ingestion and Fabric data products the pattern needs inside the segmented architecture. Build the proof of concept on Azure AI Foundry with evaluation and safety gates. Train the first crew of operators and technicians.
          • Days 61 to 90, Implement and measure: pilot on one line or one plant area with human authorization on every physical action and full audit logging. Package the pattern pack for the next plant, and report Prove metrics, downtime, scrap, and override rates, to the governance body.
